\section{Observables of interest}
\label{sec:observables}
The measured final state comprises of the 4-vectors of the lepton, $b$ jets, the reconstructed hadronic \Wboson boson and additional jets, as well as the transverse components of the missing energy. 
An extensive set of observables can be defined and measured from the final state particles, starting with a list
of basic kinematic quantities:

%\begin{table}[htbp]
%    \centering
%    \begin{tabular}{l | p{8cm}}
%      %\toprule
%      Observable                       & Description \\
%      \midrule
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{ Lepton}~\( \bm{p_T}\) and \(\bm{\eta~}\)are the transverse momentum and pseudorapidity of the single lepton, either an electron or a muon. Since the lepton rest mass is much smaller than the momentum, $\eta$ coincides with the rapidity.
\item \textbf{ Leading} and \textbf{subleading} $\bm{b}$ \textbf{jet}~\(\bm{p_T}\) are the transverse momentum of the leading and subleading $b$ jet, respectively.
\item \textbf{ Missing}~\(\bm{E_T}\) denotes the missing transverse energy. Since the initial momentum of the partonic interaction is not known along the collision axis, only the transverse component can be measured. The quantity approximates the neutrino from the leptonic \Wboson decay.
\item \textbf{ Hadronic}~\(\bm{W p_T}\) and $\bm{y}$ are the transverse momentum and rapidty of the hadronic \Wboson boson.
\item \textbf{ Leading} and \textbf{subleading additional jet } \(\bm{p_T}\) denote the transverse momentum of the leading and subleading jets, excluding the two leading $b$ jets and the jets identified as the \Wboson candidate. They correspond to the hardest and second hardest gluon radiation and are thus valuable observables for PS matching studies.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
The final state objects can be combined in various ways to approximate candidates for
leptonic or hadronically decaying top quarks, or a pseudo-\ttbar system.
Section ... describes the assignment of the two $b$ jets to either the lepton or the hadronic \Wboson boson.

\begin{itemize}
\item \( \bm{ m(bl)} \) and \( \bm{ p_T(bl) } \)
are the invariant mass and transverse momentum of the lepton and the assigned $b$ jet, while \(\bm{ \Delta R(bl)} \)
is the angular distance between the two four-vectors. The $m(bl)$ distribution is expected to be sensitive to the top quark mass below the threshold $\approx 150\GeV$. Off-shell contributions become more important in the high mass tail,
with increased sensitivity to modeling effects.
\item \( \bm{ m(bW_{had.}) } \), \( \bm{ p_T(bW_{had.}) } \) and \( \bm{ \Delta R(bW_{had.}) } \) are the equivalent observables approximating the hadronically decaying \Wboson.
\item \( \bm{ m(W_{had.}bbl) } \) and \( \bm{ p_T(W_{had.}bbl) } \) are obtained by adding the four-momenta of the hadronic \Wboson, two leading $b$ jets and the lepton.
\item When including \ETmiss in the observable definition, the quantities \( \bm{ p_T(bl\nu) } \) and
\( \bm{ p_T(W_{had.}bbl\nu) } \) can be derived to reconstruct a leptonic pseudo-top or a \ttbar system. The latter is sensitive to the strong coupling, since a non-vanishing momentum indicates a jet recoiling against the \ttbar system.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Additional observables defined to be sensitive to modeling effects, in particular to the DR/DS modeling, 
can be constructed. 
\begin{itemize}
\item \( \bm{ m^{minimax}(W_{had.}bbl)} \) Minimax observable defined in equation ....
\item \( \bm{ m^\text{min}(bl) } \) denotes the minimal invariant mass of the lepton combined with one of the $b$ jets min$(m(b_1l),m(b_2l))$.
\item The transverse mass of the leptonically decaying \Wboson boson \(\bm{m_T(W_{lep.})}\) can be obtained by adding \ETmiss and the transverse 
vector of the lepton.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Lastly, two more observables are defined, measuring jet momenta ratios.
By taking the ratio of $b$ tagged jets to non-$b$ tagged jets, uncertainties associated to
$b$ tagging cancel. 
\begin{itemize}
\item \( \bm{R_{bq} } \) is the transverse momentum ratio of the leading $b$ jet to the leading light jet, while
\item \( \bm{R_{bj} } \) denotes the scalar sum of the leading and subleading $b$ jet \pt divided by the \pt of the hadronic \Wboson.
\end{itemize}


\section{Measurement Regions}%
\label{sec:Regions}

This section presents the final event selection that is applied to define the measurement phase space.
Only events passing all selection criteria are considered in the definition of the fiducial cross section.
                                                                                         
%  \label{tab:SR_Whad_Final:yields}
%\end{table}

The selection is optimized using simulated MC event samples.
All events are required to contain exactly one signal lepton as defined in section~\ref{sec:lep_defintion}.
Prompt leptonic \( \tau \) decays are considered signal in this analysis and are therefore included.
Events are selected with a single lepton trigger and a minimum \pt requirement of \qty{30}{\GeV} is placed on the lepton.
At least three jets are expected in the signal process: two $b$ jets and at least one jet from the hadronic \Wboson decay.
The neutrino from the leptonic \Wboson boson decay introduces \met.
A cut is defined on \( \met + \mtW > \qty{60}{\GeV} \) to remove background from QCD events
with a misidentified or non-prompt lepton.
The missing transverse mass \mtW is computed from
the lepton and \met 4-vectors where the \(z\)-component is set to zero.

The \textit{Loose} region defines the most general phase space constraining the interference
effects between \ttbar and \tW processes and improve the modeling over a large kinematic region.
The explicit reconstruction of the hadronic \Wboson boson introduced in the previous section is
exploited to define the \Wboson-hadronic region.
A mass window around the \Wboson mass is defined for the \Wboson candidate $(60~\GeV < m(\Whad) < 100~\GeV)$,
as well as a minimum \pt requirement.
These cuts suppress contributions from events with poorly reconstructed \Wboson candidates.
The loss in data statistics is compensated by a significantly enhanced background suppression.
This analysis region provides a clean data sample with small background suppression.
The explicit reconstruction of the \Wboson candidates further allows the construction of
pseudo-tops from \Wboson, lepton and the two leading $b$ jets in an event.
The observables measured in this region are valuable for development and improvement of MC models.
The two analysis regions introduced above are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:RegionDefinitions}.

\section{Systematic uncertainties}

This section presents a comprehensive assessment of the systematic uncertainties that impact the 
precision of the cross-section measurement. The analysis accounts for sources of experimental uncertainty
originating from detector response, calibration and reconstruction procedure, as well as those arising
from variations in the signal and background modeling. All systematic effects are evaluated at the reconstruction
level and one-sided uncertainties are symmetrized.

\subsection{Experimental systematics uncertainties}

The reconstruction and calibration methods used in the analysis were introduced in detail in Chapter~\ref{chap:ObjectReco}. A brief overview on the associated uncertainties is given here.

\subsubsection{Leptons}

Scale factors for identification, isolation, and reconstruction that improve the agreement
between data and simulation are varied to account for uncertainties from the
calibration procedure. The same concept is applied to vary scale factors for the 
lepton trigger efficiencies.

Uncertainties associated to the understanding of energy scale and resolution as outlined in 
Section~\ref{sec:reco:el} and~\ref{sec:reco:mu} for electrons and muons respectively,
are estimated by modifying the lepton momenta. 

\subsubsection{Jets}

The comprehensive \verb|CategoryReduction| uncertainty model~\cite{web:JetSystematics},
which parametrizes the energy scale and resolution of jets is employed.
The jet energies are shifted according to the uncertainties derived
from simulation and in-situ calibration measurements.
The model includes corrections for different detector responses depending on jet momenta,
position and flavor, as well as pile-up conditions or the JVT efficiency.

\subsubsection{$b$ tagging}

The DL1r algorithm introduced in Section~\ref{sec:reco:jets} was used to identify $b$ jets.
Uncertainties related to $b$ tagging are derived and evaluated separately for 
for the $b$ tagging efficiency, as well as $c$ and light jet mis-tagging rates.
The largest contributions originate from limited statistics and modeling uncertainties in the samples used
for the calibration, as well as jet uncertainties.

\subsubsection{MET}

Uncertainties affecting lepton and jet energy measurement directly impact \ETmiss.
and are therefore directly propagated in a correlated way.

The soft term entering the calculation of \ETmiss is treated independently.
Variations in the soft term modify magnitude and direction of the \ETmiss vector.
However, this is expected to be a small effect in the analysis, since the
\ETmiss calculation is not dominated by the soft term~\cite{JETM-2020-03}.

\subsubsection{Luminosity and pile-up}

Further sources of experimental uncertainties arise from the measurement of
the integrated luminosity and the correction of the number of pile-up
collisions to data in simulated events. 
The integrated luminosity of the full Run 2 data sample after standard data-quality selection was
measured to be $140\pm 1.2~\text{fb}^{-1}$, corresponding to an uncertainty of 0.83\%~\cite{DAPR-2021-01}.

To account for pile-up mismodeling, the MC profile is reweighted to match the data. The resulting systematic uncertainty is evaluated by varying the reweighting scale factors (

possibly cite twiki: https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/viewauth/AtlasProtected/ExtendedPileupReweighting?extralog=-%20caching%20topic)

\subsection{Theory modeling uncertainties}

Theory systematics quantify uncertainties associated with the choice of event generators 
and the specific parameters employed during the simulation of the physics processes.
These uncertainties typically arise from limited theoretical accuracy in the prediction.
%These uncertainties are typically evaluated by varying the simulation parameters and
%comparing the resulting distributions to the nominal simulation. 

For the purposes of this analysis, these effects are evaluated separately for
the signal processes \ttbar and $tW$
and the modeling of the various background contributions.

\subsubsection{Cross section}

The signal consists of the independent $\ttbar$ and $tW$ samples with
normalizations to the respective absolute cross sections.
Normalization effects of the signal samples do not cancel in the
construction of the migration matrix (see Section~\ref{sec:unfolding:def})
due to the relative contributions.
An uncertainty anti-correlating the cross sections is applied
to account for possible fraction differences due to cross section uncertainties.

\subsubsection{Parton shower matching}

The parton shower algorithm is attached to the hard scatter ME
and simulates the emission of secondary and subsequent
gluon radiation.
The $p_\text{T,hard}$ parameter in the \textsc{Pythia} event generator regulates the 
definition of the vetoed region in the parton showering.
Varying this setting assesses the uncertainty associated with matching the
ME to the shower.

\subsubsection{$h_\text{damp}$}

The $h_\text{damp}$ parameter is one of the parameters controlling the ME/PS
matching in \textsc{Powheg}, effectively regulating the high-\pt radiation.
The uncertainty is evaluated by multiplying the default value
by a factor of 2~\cite{ATL-PHYS-PUB-2016-020}.

\subsubsection{Hadronization model}

In order to estimate the effect of the non-perturbative aspects of the parton shower and the hadronization,
the difference between the nominal sample to the alternative sample produced with \textsc{Powheg+Herwig} 7.1 
is defined as a systematic uncertainty. The uncertainty is symmetrized on detector level.

\subsubsection{Scales}

The renormalization scale $\mu_r$ and the factorization scale $\mu_f$
were introduced to cancel divergences in the perturbative expansion.
The scales are non-physical and should impact on the physics result.
The effect of missing higher order predictions is estimated by varying the scales
by factors of 0.5 or 2.0 in the matrix element as up and down variations.

To estimate the uncertainty associated to the final state radiation (FSR),
scales variations are also applied in the shower.
Initial state radiation (ISR) effects are assessed by varying $\alpha_S$
with the alternative Var3c A14 tune~\cite{ATL-PHYS-PUB-2017-007}. 

\subsubsection{PDF}

The PDF4LHC recommendations~\cite{Butterworth:2015oua} are followed to evaluate
the effect of different PDF sets on the cross section.
An event-by-event reweighting procedure is applied to the nominal Powheg+Pythia8 signal samples,
separately for PDF and $\alpha_S$ variations.
The variations are processed with the LHAPDF library~\cite{Buckley:2014ana} 
which obtains a combined PDF+$\alpha_S$ uncertainty.

\subsubsection{Top recoil scheme}

The radiation pattern in the PS is influenced by the gluon radiation
scheme. The nominal \textsc{Powheg+Pythia} 8 generator employs the
\verb|recoilToColor| scheme, where the second and all subsequent
gluons recoil against the $b$ quark.
This has direct implications on the $b$ jet shape, suppressing
hard out-of-cone radiation~\cite{Brooks_2019}.
Alternatively, in the \verb|recoilToTop| scheme,
top-quark itself works as recoiling object for the second and
subsequent emissions.
This option gives rise to wider-angle gluon radiation, resulting in energy deposits that do not get clustered into the b-jet.
Thus, potentially reducing the reconstructed momentum and invariant mass.

The two complementary recoil schemes introduce an additional modeling uncertainty,
evaluated by taking the difference between
the descriptions as an uncertainty and symmetrizing it.

\subsubsection{Top quark mass}

The exact value of the top quark mass directly impacts the kinematics of the 
decay products. Thus, a variation is the mass affects the number of events
passing the event selection.
Signal samples with nominal parameters, but a top quark mass of 169\GeV and 176\GeV
are employed to estimate the uncertainty. The final shift is scaled by 1/7 to
reflect the known precision of the top quark mass of 0.5\GeV~\cite{TOPQ-2017-03}.

\subsubsection{Diagram removal vs. diagram subtraction}

Special care has to be taken when merging \ttbar and $tW$ signal samples to
remove the overlap between the samples. 
The nominal \ttbar sample is combined with $tW$ samples implemented with
either the diagram removal or diagram subtraction scheme.
The uncertainty is defined as the difference between the descriptions.

\subsubsection{W mass in the reconstruction}

The reconstruction of the hadronic \Whad candidate introduced in Section...
makes explicit use of a hard-coded value for the \Wboson mass.
Using a fixed value may lead to a bias in the reconstruction due to the 
natural width of the \Wboson of $\sigma_W = 2.085\GeV$~\cite{PDG}.
An uncertainty is defined, varying the value used in the reconstruction
by $\pm 2\sigma_W$.

\subsection{Background modeling}

\subsubsection{$W$+jets}

The uncertainties assigned to the modeling of the $W+$jets background are conceptually
similar the signal samples, including the modeling uncertainties on PDF, scales
and electroweak corrections.

%Similar to the signal modeling systematics, the uncertainty in the W+ jets background is evaluated by varying the normalization factor of the MC generation parameters. These variations
%include modeling uncertainties on the cross-section, PDFs, scales and electroweak corrections.
%Table 8.7 lists the NPs associated with the W+ jets background modeling.

\subsubsection{Fake leptons}

The MC normalization uncertainty is evaluated by 
varying all samples simultaneously by $10\%$.
The control region is defined to be orthogonal to the signal region.
The effect of the exact cut values is estimated by varying the 
\ETmiss$+m_T^W$ cut to 50 and 70~\GeV, as well as demanding either at least one or at least two $b$ jets.

\subsubsection{Other background}

The “Other V” samples have scale, PDF and electroweak
uncertainties applied, while the “Other t” samples only apply a conservative 25\% normalization uncertainty.
Since the contributions from these background processes are small in the selected phase space,
the impact of the related uncertainties is expected to be negligible.


\section{Unfolding}

The distributions measured at the detector level are distorted by a variety of experimental effects, including detector acceptance, reconstruction efficiency, energy and momentum resolution, as well as reconstruction errors and misidentification of particles. 

In the \textit{unfolding} step, these detector-induced distortions are corrected to recover the underlying physical spectra from the observed data.
The differential particle-level cross sections that are obtained can be compared to test theoretical predictions probing the SM. All detector specific effects are removed, allowing the comparison of the results across measurements and experiments, as well as to future predictions which may not be available at the time of the analysis.

While the procedure of unfolding is conceptually straightforward, its practical implementation presents significant challenges. Various unfolding algorithms and software packages are available, but in this analysis, the TUnfold framework~\cite{Schmitt:2012kp} is employed due to its robustness and flexibility in handling complex detector responses.

A \textit{migration matrix} is constructed, which describes how events are redistributed across bins due to detector effects. This matrix is then inverted, adding a regularization term to stabilize the inversion process while avoiding overfitting. Every component of the systematic uncertainties is propagated individually through the unfolding procedure.

This chapter provides a comprehensive introduction to the unfolding methodology, detailing the theoretical foundations, the required input ingredients, and the practical challenges involved. It then outlines the specific unfolding strategy applied in this analysis, including the construction of the migration matrix, the choice of regularization and the propagation of uncertainties. 

%Finally, the unfolded differential cross-sections are presented, along with their associated uncertainties, and compared to theoretical predictions to assess the validity of the underlying models.

The first step of the unfolding procedure subtracts the background from the data.
The number of background events in a single bin $j$ is $N_{b,j}$.
It can be obtained from data driven methods or MC simulations, as explained in Section~\ref{sec:background}.
The remaining events are considered as signal consisting of \ttbar and $tWb$ events.

The response matrix $M_R$ encapsulates the detector response
by keeping track of event migration.
Particle level events generated in bin $i$ that end up in bin $j$ after the detector simulation
are filled in $M_{R,ij}$. An ideal measurement would therefore produce a perfectly diagonal matrix.
Due to the limited resolution in real-world experiments, off-diagonal elements may also be filled. 
Since background events were already removed in the first step, 
the response matrix is constructed from the nominal signal MC only.

The migration matrix $M_{ij}$ is obtained when normalizing the response matrix
\begin{equation}
    M_{ij} = \frac{M_{R,ij}}{N_{t,i}},
\end{equation}
where $N_{t,i}$ is the number of particle level (true) events in bin $i$.
Thus, $M_{ij}$ can be interpreted as the probability of a particle level event generated in bin $i$ to be reconstructed in bin $j$.
The choice of the bin boundaries for each observable is such that the diagonal elements of the matrix contain at least 60\% of particle level events per row.

In practice, the number of bins on reconstructed level is chosen to be twice as large, compared to the particle level.
This ensures that the $\chi^2$ minimization in the unfolding is over-constrained. 
The bin edges coincide, adding an intermediate bin on detector level to ensure convergence and a smooth distribution
at reconstruction level.

A simple inversion of $M$ in order to obtain the true particle level distribution is
not sufficient, as it can enhance statistical fluctuations in the data.

and therefore a smooth unfolded distribution.
This analysis employs the Tikhonov regularization scheme~\cite{Tikhonov1995}
as implemented in the TUnfold package~\cite{Schmitt:2019um}.

Only events passing both, the particle and reconstructed-
level selection enter the migration matrix. Therefore, two other effects need to be taken into account.

The acceptance $f^j_\text{acc}$ is defined as all events passing the detector-level selection that are also matched to particle-level, i.e. events that pass-particle and detector-level cuts, normalized to the number of reconstructed events in that bin $N_{r,j}$. A low acceptance indicates that the detector-level selection is too broad relative to the particle-level definition, leading to a mismatch between the observed and true physical phase space.
It can be calculated from the response matrix as 
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:acc}
    f^j_\text{acc} = \sum_i M_{R,ij}/N_{r,j}.
\end{equation}

The complementary efficiency 
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:eff}
    \epsilon_i = \sum_j M_{R,ij}/N_{t,i}
\end{equation}
is defined as the fraction of matched events out of all events passing the particle-level 
selection $N_{t,i}$. It corrects for events that fail to be reconstructed or selected at the detector
level. This can result from limitations in detector coverage, insufficient resolution,
trigger thresholds or reconstruction algorithms.

Lastly, the unfolded distribution is normalized by the integrated luminosity and the bin width
to obtain the differential cross section.

After putting all ingredients together, the differential fiducial cross section as a function of an observable $X$ in a given bin $i$ can be defined as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\sigma_\text{fid}}{dX_i} = \frac{1}{L\cdot \Delta X_i} \cdot \frac{1}{\epsilon_i} \cdot \sum_j f_{\text{acc}}^j M_{ij}^{-1} (N_{d,j}-N_{b,j}).
    \label{eq:Unfolding}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Propagation of uncertainties}

Uncertainty components associated with the cross section measurement are
propagated from detector level through the unfolding pipeline to unfolded cross
section level. Deviations from the nominal results are evaluated separately for
the upward and downward variation. 
The single uncertainties are assumed to be uncorrelated.
The propagated uncertainties are therefore summed up in quadrature
\begin{equation}
	\sigma_\text{tot}^\text{up/down} = \sqrt{\sum_\text{sys} (\sigma_\text{sys}^\text{up/down})^2}
\end{equation}
Depending on the nature of the uncertainty, different propagation procedures are
used, as described in the following. 
The systematic uncertainty is defined as the shift in the cross section
where the nominal result is taken from Equation~\eqref{eq:Unfolding}
\begin{equation}
	\sigma_\text{sys} = \frac{d\sigma_\text{nom}}{dX} - \frac{d\sigma_\text{sys}}{dX}
\end{equation}
The signs of the cross section shifts are kept.
Up and down variations are expected to be symmetrical around the nominal result.
One-sided uncertainties are symmetrized on detector level.

\vspace{.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Statistical and luminosity uncertainties}
\vspace{.5cm}

\noindent
The uncertainty arising from limited data statistics is estimated and propagated through the unfolding by creating Poisson smeared pseudo-experiments.
The number of events in a bin $X$ is shifted by a random number from the Poisson distribution with the mean $X$
\begin{equation}
    X_\text{shift} = X - \text{Pois}(X).
\end{equation}
Repeating this step 10000 times and evaluating the standard deviation of the toy distributions after the unfolding defines the statistical uncertainty in a given bin.

The same pseudo-experiment approach is also employed to estimate the uncertainty from the finite MC sample size.
The procedure is applied for signal and background samples.
The number of events in each bin is smeared by a Gaussian shift with mean and standard deviation equal to the yield and the uncertainty of the bin, respectively.

The uncertainty on the luminosity is propagated to the unfolded results by varying
the luminosity by 0.82\% in the simulated reconstruction level distributions.
The unfolding is then performed with the nominal corrections.

\textbf{Detector systematic uncertainties}
\vspace{.5cm}

\noindent
Detector systematics are propagated from detector to particle level by calculating varied unfolding corrections, i.e. acceptance, efficiency, migration matrix and background predictions
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\sigma_\text{sys}}{dX_i} = \frac{1}{L\cdot \Delta X_i} \cdot \frac{1}{\epsilon_i'} \cdot \sum_j {M_{ij}^{-1}}'

\end{equation}
Dashed quantities indicate the systematic variation.
The nominal data is unfolded with these varied corrections and the difference to the nominal cross section defines the uncertainty.

The parton shower uncertainty plays a dominant role in the composition of the $m(bl)$ error, with DR/DS taking over at larger values $m(bl) > 400~\GeV$,
where the $tW$ contribution becomes more important.

\vspace{.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Background modeling}
\vspace{.5cm}

\noindent
Uncertainties in the background modeling are propagated by varying the background prediction, while keeping all other unfolding corrections fixed and reapplying the unfolding procedure to the data
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\sigma_\text{sys}}{dX_i} = \frac{1}{L\cdot \Delta X_i} \cdot \frac{1}{\epsilon_i} \cdot \sum_j M_{ij}^{-1}
    f_{\text{acc}}^{j}(N_{d,j}-N_{b,j}').
\end{equation}

Uncertainties related to the background modeling only have minor contributions to the total uncertainty (see next section) and no dedicated breakdown is shown.

\vspace{.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Correlation matrices}
\vspace{.5cm}

\noindent
The covariance matrix is defined as
\begin{equation*}
\text{Cov} = 
\begin{pmatrix}
c_{11} & c_{12} & \cdots & c_{1n} \\
c_{21} & c_{22} & \cdots & c_{2n} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
c_{n1} & c_{n2} & \cdots & c_{nn}
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
where $c_{ij} = \rho_{ij} \cdot \sigma_i \cdot \sigma_j$ with the correlation coefficient $\rho_{ij}$ and
\begin{equation*}
\sigma_i = \frac{\sigma_i^{\text{up}} + \sigma_i^{\text{down}}}{2}.
\end{equation*}
$\sigma_i^\text{up/down}$ represents the up and down variations in bin $i$. 
The correlation matrix can be calculated by normalizing the covariance matrix
\begin{equation}
    \text{Corr}_{ij} = \frac{\text{Cov}_{ij}}{\sqrt{\text{Cov}_{ii} \times \text{Cov}_{jj}}}
\end{equation}
These matrices serve as a critical diagnostic tool for evaluating the validity and stability of the unfolding procedure.
While the bins are statistically independent prior to unfolding, the unfolding process inherently introduces correlations between them.

A "checkerboard pattern", characterized by strong negative correlations between adjacent bins
in combination with strong positive combinations in next to neighboring bins
indicates an over-fitting of statistical fluctuations.
The regularization is too weak, resulting in an oscillating, physically unrealistic spectrum.

Over-regularization on the other hand manifests itself by large positive correlations across the matrix.
The data is overly smoothed and biased towards the prior model.
Positive correlations across neighboring bins can also indicate that the resolution is not sufficient and
individual bins cannot be resolved.
A coarser binning would be more appropriate in that region.

The quadratic statistical correlation matrices for the four selected quantities are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:unfolding:corrma_stat}.
The self-correlation on the diagonal is by construction $1.0$.
It was taken care, that the negative correlation between neighboring bins is 
less than $0.6$. Correlations between bins rapidly decrease in the off-diagonal elements of the matrix, demonstrating a stable unfolding with appropriate choice of the regularization parameter.

The total covariance matrix can be calculated as the sum of all systematic and statistical contributions.
