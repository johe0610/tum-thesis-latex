\subsection{On the reconstruction of W bosons}

This section tackles the challenge of the reconstruction of the hadronically decaying \Wboson.
While the \Wboson decays into exactly two quarks, the signature in the detector can look quite differently.
In most cases, both decay products form jets which can be reconstructed with the standard jet clustering.
There can also be a third jet, originating from a gluon radiated from one of the quarks.
When the momentum of the \Wboson is sufficiently large, the system becomes collimated, or Lorentz-boosted.
All decay products of the \Wboson are clustered into just one jet, since the two initial quarks can no longer be resolved.
Thus, the difficulty in the reconstruction is two-fold, handling different event topologies, while solving the combinatorics arising from varying jet multiplicities.
The goal of this section is to find one reconstruction algorithm that can solve the issues, while being easy to implement.

This study employs the the nominal \ttbar sample with the loose selection described in the previous chapter.
Different jet-combinations are considered as possible candidates for the \Wboson.
The different choices can be compared by defining the purity $p$.
It is defined as matched events divided by all events passing the loose selection.
Matched events include all events where the angular distance $\Delta R$ between the true \Wboson before the decay \Wtruth
with the candidate reconstructed from jets is smaller than 0.5:
\begin{equation}
	p = \frac{\#(Events with \Delta R(\Wtruth, \Wcand)<0.5)}{\#Events}
\end{equation}
The jet combination that is selected is labeled as \Wcand.
We decided to focus on purity, instead of acceptance or efficiency, since the number of events in the analyzed data set is sufficiently large.
Therefore, we can afford to sacrifice efficiency for the benefit of a cleaner event selection.

Various jet combinations are selected as the \Wboson candidate. 
The highest purities in the low \pT regime \(\pT < 100 \GeV\) are obtained 
by selecting the dijet combination whose mass is closest to the known \Wboson mass (orange).
The performance in the medium \pT range is similar for all considered options with a purity around \(80\%\).
Only the purple curve selecting the 4-vector sum of the leading and sub-leading light jet
performs slightly poorer with a purity of around \(60\%\).
At high \Wboson momenta, a preference towards high-momenta candidates can be observed: The leading light jet (light blue) as well as the hardest dijet combination (red) perform best.
The purities obtained for the particle and detector level studies are similar, with slightly larger values obtained for the particle level study.

The same plots are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:Whadreco:masscut} with the addition of a selection cut on the mass of the \Wboson candidate \(60<m(\Wcand)<100\GeV\). This cut is applied in the nominator as well as the denominator of the purity definition.
Single bins are merged in the plotting step to reduce the statistical uncertainties.
Selecting the jet combination whose mass is closest to the true \Wboson mass, is not the best choice as \Wcand (orange curve).
The highest purity is obtained by selecting the dijet system containing leading and sub-leading light jet (purple).
At highest \Wboson momenta, selecting the leading jet results in the highest purity.
The statistical uncertainties are significantly smaller, compared to the other options.
It becomes evident, that one should pre-select different jet combinations as preliminary candidates to account for the different topologies from resolved and boosted events.

Figure~\ref{fig:Whadreco:combined} compares two different pre-selections.
The blue curve labelled "1-,2-,3-jet" a total of five objects are pre-selected: the leading jet, the subleading jet, the vectorial sum of the two 4-vectors, the leading dijet and the leading 3-jet combination. The "1-,2-jet" algorithm displayed in red drops the last two candidates from the list.
While both options produce satisfactory results, the highest purity is obtained with the latter algorithm, when selecting a mass window around the \Wboson mass. This is the final algorithm that will be used in the analysis for event selection as well as observable definitions. The purity exceeds $90\%$ for \Wboson momenta larger than 120\GeV.

A pseudo-\Wboson can be found for each event.
Selection cuts can be applied on \Wcand. The mass cut $60<m(\Wcand)<100$ GeV is motivated by the purity studies.
An additional cut on \pT(\Whad) will be applied to ensure a good quality of the reconstruction for each event.
A \(\pT > 80\GeV\) cut-off was chosen to ensure, that the purity exceeds \(60\%\) for the particle and detector level reconstruction.

The results obtained from the classical reconstruction approach are compared to a Machine-Learning based reconstruction.
A fully-connected classification neural network is designed and trained for that purpose.
The five leading light jets are considered for that study.
Every 1-, 2- and 3-jet combination built from the input jets is considered as a \Wboson candidate.
This results in a total of 25 combinations.
The 4-vectors of the jet combinations are passed as input to the network.
The jet combination with the smallest distance to \Wtruth in \(\Delta R\) is labeled with "1", while "0" is assigned to all other combinations as training label.
The network consists of five layers with 8, 16, 32, 16, 8 nodes, respectively.
This results in a total of 1401 trainable parameters.
