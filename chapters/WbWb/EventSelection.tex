\section{Baseline Event selection}%
\label{sec:EventSelection}

The phase space regions of interest, also known as signal regions, are defined by the complex final state topology of the \WbWb process.
The signal includes all processes yielding a \WbWb final state, with dominant contributions
from \ttbar and \tW production.
Representative Feynman diagrams of the two dominant process are depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:wbwb:feynman}.
Contributions from diboson production in association with a $b$ quark are found to be negligible.

This analysis measures the semi-leptonic final state, in which one \Wboson boson decays leptonically and the other decays hadronically.
The final state is therefore defined by:
\begin{equation*}
    pp \rightarrow WbWb \rightarrow \ell\nu q \bar{q}' b \bar{b}
\end{equation*}
The lepton is either an electron or a muon and may also originate from a subsequent $W\rightarrow\tau\nu_\tau\rightarrow l\nu_l\nu_\tau$ decay.
In order to enhance the signal process and reduce background contamination, a variety of kinematic requirements (cuts) are applied.
The event selection is optimized using simulated MC event samples.

The data will be corrected for detector resolution and acceptance effects in the \textit{unfolding} step (see Section~\ref{sec:unfolding}).
This step requires the definition of an unfolding target.
The unfolding target corresponds to the phase space definition on particle level.
In order to keep acceptance and efficiency corrections as small as possible, the particle level phase space is defined as close to the detector level phase space
as possible. This means, that whenever possible, the same cuts are applied on particle and detector level, keeping
extrapolation into phase spaces that cannot be measured to a minimum.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Exactly one detector level signal lepton, as defined in Table~\ref{tab:ObjReco:Electrons} (electrons) or Table~\ref{tab:ObjReco:Muons} (muons) is required.
    Events are selected with a single lepton trigger. An additional $\pt > 30~\GeV$ cut is applied on the identified lepton.
    While the detector level electrons need to fulfill $|\eta|<1.37$ and $1.52<|\eta|<2.47$, the full range $|\eta| < 2.5$ is employed on particle level.
    \item Since the neutrino is undetected, significant \ETmiss is expected in the event.
    This is combined with the transverse mass of leptonically decaying \Wboson boson $m_T^W$ computed from the signal lepton and
    \ETmiss 4-vectors where the $z$-component is set to zero.
    The final cut at $\ETmiss + m_T^W > 60 \GeV$ aims to suppress multi-jet background with a misidentified or non-prompt lepton.
    \item At least three jets are required, reconstructed with the anti-$k_t$ clustering and a jet radius parameter of $R=0.4$, as defined in Table~\ref{tab:ObjReco:Jets}.
    They need to fulfill $p_T > 25 \GeV$ and $|\eta|<2.5$. At least two of them have to be identified as originating from a $b$ quark, corresponding to a $b$ tag at WP $77\%$ on detector level and cone matching, as described in Section~\ref{sec:ObjReco:Part}, on particle level.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
This baseline selection defines the \textit{loose signal region} and is applied to all events.
The properties of the hadronically decaying \Wboson boson have not been exploited so far in the event selection.
In the next section, a procedure is derived for the explicit reconstruction of a \Whad candidate from jets.
