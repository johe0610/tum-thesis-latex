\section{Cross section results}

This section presents the unfolded single differential particle level cross section results.
An extensive set of observables which was defined in section~\ref{sec:observables}, is presented in Figures~~\ref{fig:whadpart:xsec:selected}, \ref{fig:whadpart:xsec:basic} and~\ref{fig:whadpart:xsec:combined}. 
The data are compared to various predictions obtained from MC event generators. 
Powheg interfaced with the Pythia parton shower is shown in the DR and DS schemes,
alongside Powheg employing the alternative Herwig parton shower.
The data points show statistical uncertainties as black error bars, in addition to the total error as a gray band.
The error breakdown is provided in figures~\ref{fig:unfolding:sys}, \ref{fig:whadpart:sys:basic} and~\ref{fig:whadpart:sys:combined}.

The unfolding procedure was illustrated with the four observables lepton \pt, leading $b$ jet \pt, \pt of the hadronic \Wboson and $m(bl)$.
The absolute cross sections and the error breakdown are displayed in figures~\ref{fig:whadpart:xsec:selected} and~\ref{fig:unfolding:sys}.

The PH+H7 prediction provides the best description of the overall normalization,
while PH+PY8 predictions overestimate the spectra by a few percent.
The MC models predict a harder \pt spectrum , reflected by an overestimation of the data at large momenta. This can be traced back to missing higher-orders in the predictions. Predictions at NNLO in QCD relax the tension. (SOURCE)

The ratio of the PH+PY8 predictions with respect to the data in the $m(bl)$ bulk region ($m(bl)<150\GeV$) is flat with an offset of $\approx 8\%$.
Above the kinematic threshold, discrepancies between DR and DS descriptions can be observed. While the DR prediction agrees with the data within uncertainties, the DS scheme underestimates the data by $20\%$.
PH+H7 provides the best description of the $m(bl)$ distribution, agreeing with the data over a large kinematic range.

Figure~\ref{fig:unfolding:sys} demonstrates that detector uncertainties are the major error source, with statistical uncertainties increasing in the tails of the distributions. Variations in the background modeling do not contribute significantly, while variations in the signal modeling become more important in the high \pt / mass range.

Keeping the sign of the cross section shift when assessing uncertainties, allows a direct comparison of up and down variation.
The uncertainties are symmetrical over the full observable ranges.

The agreement between measured cross sections $\sigma_\text{data}$ and theory predictions $\sigma_\text{theo}$ can be quantified with a $\chi^2$ test
\begin{equation}
    \chi^2 = (\sigma_\text{data}-\sigma_\text{theo})^T~\text{Cov}^{-1}~(\sigma_\text{data}-\sigma_\text{theo})
\end{equation}
where the uncertainties and correlations between bins after the unfolding is encapsulated by the inverse covariance matrix Cov$^{-1}$.
Uncertainties in the predictions are not included in the calculation.
The number of degrees of freedom NDF is given by the number of bins.

The $p$-value gives the probability of observing a
$\chi^2$ value at least as extreme as the one calculated, under the assumption that the null hypothesis is true (i.e., the theoretical model is consistent with the data).

The $\chi^2/$NDF and the $p$-values are provided in Table~\ref{tab:chisq} for the three considered theory predictions and every measured observable.
