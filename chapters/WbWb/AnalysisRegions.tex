\chapter{Measurement Regions}%
\label{ch:regions}

The analysis focuses on three kinematic regions: a large phase space
to constrain the interference effects between \ttbar and \tW processes
and improve the modelling over a large kinematic region,
a dedicated phase space at high scales to improve the modelling for future searches,
and a dedicated measurement of the \WbWb production cross section where
the \Wboson boson is directly reconstructed.
The bulk interference region, defined in \cref{sec:regions-bulk}, aims for
the loosest possible selection in the \WbWb phase-space and focuses
on observables sensitive to the interference. The search-like interference
region, defined in \cref{sec:regions-search}, applies additional selection criteria
inspired by BSM searches aiming for as large as possible sensitivity
to the interference. Finally the \Wboson-hadronic measurement region, presented in \cref{sec:regions-wbwb}, focuses on the
overall modelling of this final state and directly considers the \Wboson bosons and \bjets for the definition of the phase space,
which requires the reconstruction of the \Wboson boson (without constraint on intermediate top quarks).
All the region share the same baseline cuts and the summary of the event selection is in \cref{tab:RegionDefinitions}.
The cutflow tables for these selections are available in \cref{app:cutflows}.

\section{Baseline Cuts}%
\label{sec:BaselineCuts}
The selection is optimised using simulated MC event samples.
All events are required to contain exactly one signal lepton as defined
in \cref{ch:definitions}. This includes  prompt leptonic \( \tau \) decays, which are considered signal in this analysis.
 Events are selected with a single lepton trigger
and a minimum \pt requirement of \qty{30}{\GeV} is placed on the lepton.
Furthermore the expected number of jets in the
signal is 4 (2 jets from top quark decays and 2 from \Wboson boson decays),
so a preselection condition of at least 4 baseline jets and at least 2 \bjets
in the event is applied. For the \Wboson-hadronic selection this is relaxed to 3 jets
as also a single jet can be reconstructed as the hadronically decaying \Wboson boson.
The neutrino from the second \Wboson boson decay introduces \met.
This allows a cut on \( \met + \mtW > \qty{60}{\GeV} \)
to be applied to remove the QCD background, which in this case enters
the selection as a mis-identified or non-prompt lepton.
The \mtW is defined as the transverse mass computed from
the lepton and \met 4-vectors where the \(z\)-component is set to 0.



\section{Interference sensitivity metrics}

To evaluate if an observable is sensitive to the interference between
\tW and \ttbar two metrics have been defined. 
% First a large difference between DR and DS simulated \tW samples is required:
The first metric measures the difference between DR and DS simulated \tW samples, such that:

\begin{equation}
  \dfrac{N_\mathrm{DS}}{N_\mathrm{DR}}
  \label{eq:metric:DRDS}
\end{equation}
where \( N_\mathrm{DR} \)(\( N_\mathrm{DS} \)) is the number of events passing the selection from the single-top DR\,(DS) samples, respectively.  % chktex 36
Additionally, a separation of up to \qty{20}{\%} between the DR and DS samples is observed in the bulk interference region in most of distributions.
In the search-like interference region, a difference of up to \qty{50}{\%} is observed.
As our modelling of the \tW sample differs, measuring the quantities sensitive to it will help tuning the generators.
This metric only leads to phase spaces where the modelling of \tW is inaccurate. Overall any region where the fraction
of \tW contribution is large, should be measured so a second metric is defined as the fraction of \tW in the signal:

\begin{equation}
  \dfrac{N(\tW)}{N(\tW) + N(\ttbar)}\,,
  \label{eq:metric:ratio}
\end{equation}
In the bulk interference region, a separation of up to \qty{20}{\%} is required between DR and DS samples throughout each distribution.
Additionally, in the search-like interference region, a separation of up to \qty{60}{\%} is necessary.
In addition to the two metrics described in \cref{eq:metric:DRDS,eq:metric:ratio} an additional metric is used for optimising the event selection.
This metric is defined as the difference between the purity of DR and DS,
such that:

\begin{equation}
  FOM = \dfrac{N_\mathrm{DR}}{N_\mathrm{total-simulated(DR)}} - \dfrac{N_\mathrm{DS}}{N_\mathrm{total-simulated(DS)}}
  \label{eq:PurityofDRDS}
\end{equation}

where \(N_\mathrm{total-simulated(DR)}\) and \(N_\mathrm{total-simulated(DS)}\) 
are the total of all simulated events including single-top DR and DS, respectively.  % chktex 36
This metric was used to quantitatively optimise search-like region while keeping sufficient statistics.


\section{Discriminants}
To suppress a large fraction of semi-leptonic \ttbar and SM background,
dominated by \Wjets, a number of discriminating variables have been
selected in addition to the preselection.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\mblmin}{Minimum invariant mass of a lepton and a b-jet}}
The observable that is most sensitive to the interference
between double and single top production is the invariant mass of
a \bjet and a lepton. An ambiguity is present when pairing the correct
\bjet to the lepton. The solution which provides
the highest sensitivity is:

\begin{equation}
  \mblmin \equiv \min \left\{ m_{b_i \ell} \right\}  % chktex 21
  \label{eq:mblmin}
\end{equation}

where the \(b_i\) goes over all \bjets in the event.
Due to suppression of the doubly resonant contribution at high values
of \mblmin, the differential cross-section above this kinematic endpoint
of \qty{150}{\GeV} has increased sensitivity to interference effects.

\subsubsection{ \( \Delta R (b_{1}, b_{2}) \) }
An additional cut can be introduced on \( \Delta R (b_{1}, b_{2}) \)
to further reduce the \ttbar contribution. It can evade the \mblmin kinematic
bound if the two \btagged jets used in the reconstruction do not originate
from the two top decays, but instead from the \(b\)- and \(c\)-quarks from
a hadronic top decay. In this scenario \( \Delta R (b_{1}, b_{2}) \)
is expected to be small and can be suppressed by requiring
a minimum separation between \btagged jets.

\section{Bulk Interference Region}%
\label{sec:regions-bulk}

On top of the baseline selection only minimal additional cuts have
been investigated. Those are jet multiplicities and baseline jet
momentum selection. Optimisations of the selection are presented in more
detail in \cref{app:optimisation-bulk}. The jet multiplicity selection
stayed at the baseline condition at least 4 jets of which at least 2 should be tagged
as \bjets (pending unfolding optimisations). Several values of baseline
jet \pt have also been tested: \qty{20}{\GeV}, \qty{25}{\GeV}, \qty{30}{\GeV}
and \qty{50}{\GeV}. While minor reduction of uncertainty is expected
for low momenta the tails of distributions sensitive to the interference
do not show a significant difference between individual cut values.
The value of \qty{25}{\GeV} has been chosen as the baseline jet \pt requirement.

Sensitive variables were defined by evaluating the sensitivity metrics
described above on the baseline selection. They can be split into two categories:
basic quantities describing individual particles and more complex compound quantities
where the sensitivity to the \tW and \ttbar interference is targeted.
Out of basic quantities, only lepton \pt and leading \bjet \pt show a high
fraction of \tW events in the signal and a large DR-DS difference,
while the other basic variables do not.
They are shown on \cref{fig:SR_Bulk_Final_l_4j_incl_blinded:basic}.
The yields in the bulk interference region are listed in
\cref{tab:SR_Bulk_Final:yields}.

Several compound variables have been defined. Missing transverse momentum is used
as defined in \cref{sec:definitions-met}. The fraction of \tW events is increasing
towards higher values.

The symmetric \(m_{\mathrm{T}2}\) variable is defined as
\begin{equation}
  m_{\mathrm{T}2}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},1}, \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},2},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}) = \min_{{ \mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{T},1}+\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{T},2}=\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}}[\max(m_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},1},\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{T},1}),m_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},2},\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{T},2}))],
\end{equation}
where \(m_{\mathrm{T}}\) is the transverse mass, \(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},1}\) and  \(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},2}\) indicate the transverse momentum vectors of two particles of the same mass and \met is the missing transverse momentum vector.
A minimisation is performed over all possible combination of the transverse momentum vectors \(\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{T},1}\) and \(\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{T},2}\) that sum up to the missing transverse momentum vector \(\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}\).
It is used to measure masses of particles pair produced at hadron colliders,
where each particle decays to one particle that is directly observable and another
particle whose existence can only be inferred from missing transverse momenta.
This variable is closely related to the transverse mass variable.
It can be generalised for the asymmetric case where one of the equal-massed particle decays fully visibly
and the other decay involves an invisible particle, as is the case for \ttbar in this analysis~\cite{Lester:2014yga}.
\begin{equation}
  am_{\mathrm{T}2}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},b1}, \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},b2},\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},\ell},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}) = \min[m_{\mathrm{T}2}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},b1}+\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},\ell}, \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},b2},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}),m_{\mathrm{T}2}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},b1}, \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},b2}+\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T},\ell},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}})]
\end{equation}

A simplified case is just to look at the \mbl variable. As multiple \bjets
are present in the event \mblmin and \mblmax can be defined, where \mblmin
selects the leptonically decaying top quark from the \ttbar process well, while in the \tW case a much
larger fraction is also expected for high mass values. A limitation is a large background contamination.
Similarly \mtblmin and \mtblmax can be defined as combinations of a lepton, \bjet
and \met that give the minimum and maximum values of transverse mass computed
with these objects. While the behaviour of \mtblmin and \mtblmax is similar compared to 
\mblmin and \mblmax, the signal contribution is
smeared more and a better sensitivity due to lower background contamination
is expected for the tail of the distributions.
The \mblmax and \mtblmax variables have lower sensitivity to the interference
and are not used as part of this analysis.

of \met,  \mtW, asymmetric \amttwo, \mtblmin and \mblmin.

\mbb, \ptbb, \deltaRbb and \deltaRbbl. These are shown

\section{Search-Like Interference Region}%
\label{sec:regions-search}

Besides looking at the bulk selection the aim is to investigate also
a phase space inspired by BSM searches that is particularly sensitive
to the interference between \ttbar and \tW, and where the modelling
approaches can be studied in a region that is particularly
important for BSM searches.
This choice is driven by substantial uncertainties already present
in the search phase space, particularly those pertaining to the theoretical
modelling of single-top events. For example, SUSY searches or searches looking
for Dark Matter~\cite{EXOT-2021-01}. The aim of conducting this study is
to facilitate a more precise calibration of theoretical models related
to single-top processes, ultimately leading to a reduction of associated
uncertainties.
Initial sensitivity analyses and event selection optimizations were carried out,
employing two distinct approaches:
The first utilises a high \met cut, while the second involves a high lepton
\pt cut (see \cref{app:optimisation-searchlike} for details).
The result of these investigations revealed that both variables had a substantial
influence to discriminate difference between the interference
modelling with the DR or DS scheme.\@ Hence, an event selection was
formulated by merging the high \met and high lepton \pt cuts, with the condition
that \met is a linear function of the lepton \pt, such that:

\begin{equation}
  \met > -A\pt\left(\ell\right) + C
\end{equation}

Where \(A\) and \(C\) represent the gradient and the intercept, respectively.
The cut is set at \( \met > -0.625\pt(\ell) + \qty{270}{\GeV} \).
The optimisation of the selection is described in detail
in \cref{app:optimisation-searchlike}.

On top of the baseline selection, other cuts are introduced to discriminate
single-top from \ttbar and \Wjets:
\begin{itemize}
  \item  \( \mblmin \) variable was chosen to discriminate single-top from \( \ttbar \). The cut applied was set at \( \mblmin > \qty{150}{\GeV} \).
  \item \( \Delta R (b_{1}, b_{2}) \) variable was chosen to further reduce the \(\ttbar \) background. The cut applied was set at \( \Delta R (b_{1}, b_{2})  > 1.4\).
  \item Leading \bjet \pt variable was chosen to reduce the \ttbar and \Wjets background. This was set to \( \pt(b_{1}) > \qty{160}{\GeV} \).
  \item 4-jet inclusive selection was preferred over a 3-jet inclusive selection, due to a reduction of the \Wjets background.
  \item 2-\bjet inclusive selection was preferred to a 2-\bjet exclusive selection, due to a reduction of the \Wjets background.
\end{itemize}

The yields in the search-like interference region are listed in
\cref{tab:SR_Searches_Triangular_l_4j_incl:yields}.
Sensitive variables to discriminate predictions using the DR or DS are shown in
\cref{fig:SR_Searches_Triangular_l_4j_incl_blinded:main,fig:SR_Searches_Triangular_l_4j_incl_blinded:compound}.
Furthermore variables related to the two \bjets are shown in
\cref{fig:SR_Searches_Triangular_l_4j_incl_blinded:bb}.

More variables, which are not to be unfolded, are shown for their agreement of data and MC are
in \cref{fig:SR_Searches_Triangular_l_4j_incl:additional1,fig:SR_Searches_Triangular_l_4j_incl:additional2}.
