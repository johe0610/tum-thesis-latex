\subsection{Top quark physics}


The top quark is the heaviest known elementary particle in the Standard Model, with a mass of approximately 173\GeV. This exceptional mass imparts unique properties to the top quark, making its study a cornerstone of the physics program at the LHC, providing a critical testing ground for the SM.

Due to the strong suppression of other decay modes by the CKM matrix elements, the top quark decays almost exclusively into a
\Wboson boson and a
$b$ quark. Consequently,
\ttbar final states are categorized by the decay modes of the two
\Wboson bosons. The all-hadronic channel, in which both
W bosons decay into quarks, possesses the largest branching fraction at approximately 46\%. This is followed closely by the single-lepton (or lepton+jets) channel at 45\%, and finally the dilepton channel, which has the smallest contribution.
The relative contributions of the \ttbar
final states are summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:tt_xsec_summary}.








While the ATLAS and CMS collaborations have already measured single and double top-quark production cross-sections in good agreement with theoretical predictions, further precision measurements are essential to probe the nature of the electroweak sector.
