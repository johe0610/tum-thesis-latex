The top quark is the heaviest known elementary particle and
plays a pivotal role in probing the limits of the Standard Model (SM).
The final state defined by two $W$ bosons and two $b$ quarks is of particular interest. 
It arises predominantly from top-quark pair production (\ttbar), with significant contributions
from single-top production in association with a $W$ boson ($tW$). 
Precise measurements of its differential cross-section provide a stringent test of SM predictions and are valuable
for developing improved theoretical models.
This thesis presents the first differential cross-section measurement of the $WbWb$ final state in the semileptonic channel, 
where one $W$ boson decays leptonically and the other decays hadronically. The analysis is performed using the full 
ATLAS Run 2 dataset, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 140 fb$^{-1}$, collected at a center-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s} = 13 \TeV$.
The cross sections are unfolded to particle level, correcting the data for detector effects and inefficiencies,
thus providing a model-independent measurement that can be directly compared to theoretical predictions.
The correction procedure is explained in detail with the example of the jet flavor tagger calibration.
The measurement is performed in a fiducial phase space. The hadronic $W$ boson is explicitly reconstructed from light-flavor jets,
allowing for the determination of the full kinematics of the $WbWb$ system.
The results are compared to state-of-the-art SM predictions from a variety of Monte Carlo generators.
%The derived cross sections are further used to determine the top quark mass.
By providing a model-independent measurement of the $WbWb$ cross section with high precision, this thesis delivers valuable input
for future theoretical and experimental studies, including the development of improved MC generators and the exploration of potential new physics phenomena
beyond the SM.
