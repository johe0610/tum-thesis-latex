The top quark is the heaviest known elementary particle in the Standard Model (SM).
It plays a leading role in testing the electroweak sector and probing the limits of the SM.
Of particular interest is the final state defined by two $W$ bosons and two $b$ quarks. 
This final state arises predominantly from top-quark pair production (\ttbar), with significant contributions
from single-top production in association with a $W$ boson ($tW$). 
In conventional Monte Carlo (MC) event generators, these processes are treated independently.
Thus, interference effects between \ttbar and $tW$
amplitudes are not fully captured, resulting in non-trivial modeling uncertainties.
In addition, the $WbWb$ final state is also an interesting SM process in its own right, 
as it provides a direct probe of the top-quark  mass and width
as well as the strong coupling constant $\alpha_s$ and Parton Distribution Functions (PDFs) of the proton.

This thesis presents the first differential cross-section measurement of the $WbWb$ final state in the semileptonic channel, 
where one $W$ boson decays leptonically and the other decays hadronically. The analysis is performed using the full 
ATLAS Run 2 dataset, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 140 fb$^{-1}$, collected at a center-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s} = 13 \TeV$.
The cross sections are unfolded to particle level, providing a model-independent measurement that can be directly compared to theoretical predictions.
The measurement is performed in a fiducial phase space. The hadronic $W$ boson is explicitly reconstructed from light-flavor jets,
allowing for a more precise determination of the full kinematics of the $WbWb$ system.
The results are compared to state-of-the-art SM predictions from a variety of Monte Carlo generators.
%The derived cross sections are further used to determine the top quark mass.
By providing a model-independent measurement of the $WbWb$ cross section with high precision, this thesis delivers a valuable benchmark
for future theoretical and experimental studies, including the development of improved MC generators and the exploration of potential new physics phenomena
beyond the SM.
